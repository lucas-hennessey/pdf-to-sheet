PDF to Sheet

First-time setup: connect your Google account (about 10 minutes, free)

You only do this once. It lets this app add rows to your sheet as you. The sheet stays private and nothing is shared publicly. Google's screens change now and then, so wording may differ slightly.

  1. Open Google Cloud Console. Go to console.cloud.google.com and sign in with the Google account that owns (or can edit) your sheet. Accept the terms if asked. No credit card or billing is needed.
  2. Create a project. Click the project menu at the top, then New project. Name it PDF to Sheet and click Create. Make sure it's selected at the top.
  3. Turn on the Sheets API. Open this page and click Enable.
  4. Set up the consent screen. Open Google Auth Platform and click Get started. App name: PDF to Sheet. Support email: your email. Audience: External. Contact email: your email. Tick the agreement box and click Create.
  5. Add yourself as a test user. In the left menu choose Audience, then under Test users click Add users, enter the Gmail address you'll sign in with, and Save. Skipping this causes an "access blocked" error later.
  6. Create the key file. In the left menu choose Clients, then Create client. Application type: Desktop app. Name: PDF to Sheet. Click Create, then Download JSON.
  7. Give the file to this app. Click the button below and choose the file you just downloaded (it's usually in Downloads and named client_secret_….json).
  8. Sign in. Click Sign in with Google below. Google will say "Google hasn't verified this app." That's expected because it's your own app. Click Advanced, then Go to PDF to Sheet (unsafe), then Continue and allow access to Google Sheets.
  9. Choose your sheet. Paste the sheet's URL and tab name below and click Save & test connection.

Good to know: while the app is in Google's "testing" mode, your sign-in expires after 7 days. When that happens the app will tell you, and you just click Sign in with Google again (steps 1 to 7 are not repeated). The app only asks for access to Google Sheets, not your other files.
